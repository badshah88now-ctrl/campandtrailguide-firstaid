Trail First Aid: Building a Kit You Can Actually Use

A first aid kit is the piece of gear you hope never to open — and the one you will be glad you packed when you need it. The best kit is not the biggest one; it is the one you know how to use.

Start with the basics: adhesive bandages in several sizes, gauze pads, medical tape, antiseptic wipes, tweezers for splinters and ticks, and any personal medication. Add blister treatment — moleskin or blister plasters — because blisters are the most common trail injury by far.

Include a few items people forget: a small pair of scissors, safety pins, a triangular bandage that doubles as a sling, and pain relief tablets. A tick remover and a small mirror help you check hard to see spots after walking through long grass.

Know how to use what you carry. A kit is useless if nobody in the group knows how to clean a wound or improvise a splint. Take a basic wilderness first aid course before a long trip, and practise opening the kit with cold hands — fine motor skills disappear when you are cold or stressed.

Keep the kit accessible, not buried at the bottom of your pack. A dry bag or zip-lock bag keeps it dry, and a bright colour makes it easy to find in a hurry. Check it before every trip and replace anything used or expired.

Tell someone your route and expected return time before you set out, and carry a charged phone or a personal locator beacon on remote trails.
